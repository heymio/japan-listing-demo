# Localization planning

## Purpose

Locale work converts evidence-backed strategy into language and framing appropriate to the target locale without inventing needs from the market label alone.

## Locale boundary

For `ja-JP`, use current Japanese market/channel evidence and natural Japanese copy conventions. Do not assume a preference, pain point, or scenario merely because the project market is Japan.

Market and locale remain separate: a Japan-market project does not automatically force `ja-JP`, and Japanese copy rules do not establish a market strategy.

## What localization may change

Localization may adapt:

- terminology and category language;
- sentence structure and tone;
- shopper questions and objection framing;
- search language supported by current evidence;
- examples and scenarios supported by market/channel evidence;
- information density and emphasis where channel/local expectations are evidenced.

## What localization may not change

Localization may not alter product fact, offer scope, claim readiness, measured performance, test conditions, or channel capability.

## ja-JP copy QA

Check every consumer-visible line for:

- native word order and particle use;
- sentence completeness;
- consistent terminology;
- channel-appropriate politeness and tone;
- clear subject/benefit relationship;
- concise mobile-readable headlines;
- correct product, feature, and service naming;
- consistent loanwords, abbreviations, and technical terms;
- claim conditions attached to the statement they qualify;
- removal of internal planning language and untranslated placeholders.

Literal translation is not localization. Rewrite from the approved meaning while preserving facts, conditions, hierarchy, and proof.

## Numbers and formats

Verify the current project/channel conventions for:

- currency and tax display;
- date and time formats;
- decimals and thousands separators;
- measurement units;
- full-width / half-width characters;
- punctuation and spacing;
- model numbers, trademarks, and symbols.

Channel conventions override generic locale habits when they are current and verified.

## Search-language research

Use current evidence to review, when relevant:

- kanji / hiragana / katakana / loanword variants;
- spacing and no-spacing variants;
- category + use-intent phrasing;
- problem-led and feature-led phrasing;
- channel-specific query behavior.

Do not convert a translation glossary into a keyword strategy. Record source, date, intent, and allowed usage in the Market Evidence Registry.

## Native review

Before formal release, require native review for headline naturalness, ambiguity, tone, terminology, legal/claim wording, mobile comprehension, and spoken-language elements when applicable.

Machine-generated or non-native copy remains `PROVISIONAL COPY` until this review is complete.

## Evidence

Keep locale evidence traceable to current sources. Separate stable language conventions from volatile market/search behavior.

## Output

Produce a Localization Brief that informs Message Architecture and the Creative Strategy Kernel. Production receives only the locale implications relevant to the current asset, not the entire localization research history.

# Market, VOC, and competitor research

## Research equation

```text
Product / Category × Market × Locale × Channel × Current project evidence
```

Do not start from national stereotypes or permanent category assumptions. `JP` identifies the target market; it does not prove a need, preference, scene, keyword, visual convention, or message priority.

## Evidence types

Use and label current evidence such as:

1. official government, regulator, standards, or platform sources;
2. current channel documentation and account capabilities;
3. industry or retailer data;
4. search and keyword evidence;
5. competitor pages and advertising;
6. verified reviews, VOC, support data, and user research;
7. social/community signals when appropriate.

## Evidence versus inference

Separate evidence from inference. Record source date, scope, confidence, and permitted use. A competitor execution proves what that competitor currently does; it does not prove consumer preference or the current project's account capability.

## Market Evidence Registry

For every reusable or project-specific market conclusion, record:

- source and date;
- market and locale;
- category, channel, audience, and offer scope;
- evidence type;
- neutral finding;
- confidence;
- allowed usage such as strategy, copy, keyword research, visual brief, claim, comparison, or internal review;
- expiry / refresh trigger.

Conflicting findings remain separate until resolved; do not average them into one consumer claim.

## VOC

VOC should be collected as an independent planning input rather than used only to validate a predetermined message. Group recurring needs, objections, anxieties, desired outcomes, and terminology while preserving source traceability.

When sample-level evidence is available, preserve enough metadata to audit interpretation: source/channel, date, rating or sentiment where relevant, purchase status where available, and the original-language wording needed for language analysis.

## Competitor analysis

Compare direct and adjacent competitors on positioning, shopper questions answered, proof style, content architecture, offer structure, and channel execution. Distinguish factual observations from strategic interpretation.

A competitor page may evidence the competitor's message sequence, visible slots, proof objects, copy language, and current execution pattern. It cannot prove the current project's product facts, account permissions, or a general market preference.

## Search and keyword research

Generate search-language work from:

```text
current category
× current channel
× requested locale
× project evidence
× current search data
```

Review feature-led, problem-led, use-intent, category, spelling/script, spacing, and loanword variants when supported by current data. Record query source, date, intent, match/source type, and allowed usage. Do not turn a translation glossary into a keyword strategy.

## Visual localization

Visual localization must be derived from the current audience, category, offer, use context, channel, and evidence. Proposed homes, rooms, people, objects, density, styling, and interaction patterns remain provisional until supported by project evidence or human review.

Do not infer a generic "Japanese look" from the country label alone. Convert evidence-backed environment and behavior implications into the Creative Strategy Kernel, then project only what the current asset needs.

## Dynamic information

Reverify changing items such as channel modules, prices, laws, certification, availability, subscriptions, product versions, competitor pages, and search behavior.

## Planning outputs

Create a Market Evidence Registry that can feed Consumer Strategy, Message Architecture, Channel Planning, and the Creative Strategy Kernel. Preserve deep reasoning inside Planning, but do not carry the full research corpus into Production once decisions are made.

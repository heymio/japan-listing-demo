# Market, VOC, and competitor research

## Research equation

```text
Product / Category × Market × Locale × Channel × Current project evidence
```

Do not start from national stereotypes or permanent category assumptions.

## Evidence types

Use and label current evidence such as:

1. official government, regulator, standards, or platform sources;
2. current channel documentation and account capabilities;
3. industry or retailer data;
4. search and keyword evidence;
5. competitor pages and advertising;
6. verified reviews, VOC, support data, and user research;
7. social/community signals when appropriate.

## Evidence versus inference

Separate evidence from inference. Record source date, scope, confidence, and permitted use. A competitor execution proves what that competitor currently does; it does not prove consumer preference or the current project's account capability.

## VOC

VOC should be collected as an independent planning input rather than used only to validate a predetermined message. Group recurring needs, objections, anxieties, desired outcomes, and terminology while preserving source traceability.

## Competitor analysis

Compare direct and adjacent competitors on positioning, shopper questions answered, proof style, content architecture, and channel execution. Distinguish factual observations from strategic interpretation.

## Dynamic information

Reverify changing items such as channel modules, prices, laws, certification, availability, subscriptions, product versions, competitor pages, and search behavior.

## Planning outputs

Create a Market Evidence Registry that can feed Consumer Strategy, Message Architecture, and the Creative Strategy Kernel. Do not carry the full research corpus into Production once decisions are made.

# ja-JP localization

## Activation

Use this file only when the requested consumer locale is:

```text
ja-JP
```

A Japan target market does not automatically force Japanese copy. The Project Definition locale controls the output language.

## Internal and consumer languages

- Internal analysis and QA follow the user's working language unless the project specifies otherwise.
- Consumer-facing copy follows the requested locale.
- Source quotations preserve the source language.
- Cross-market comparison may use one analysis language while retaining original-language evidence.

## Copy QA

Check every consumer-visible line for:

- native word order and particle use;
- sentence completeness;
- consistent terminology;
- appropriate level of politeness for the channel and brand;
- clear subject and benefit relationship;
- concise headlines that remain understandable on mobile;
- correct product, feature, and service naming;
- consistent handling of loanwords, abbreviations, and technical terms;
- conditions attached to the claim they qualify;
- removal of internal planning language and untranslated placeholders.

Do not treat literal translation as localization. Rewrite from the approved meaning while preserving facts, conditions, hierarchy, and proof.

## Numbers and formats

Verify project requirements for:

- currency display;
- tax inclusion wording;
- date format;
- time format;
- decimal and thousands separators;
- measurement units;
- full-width and half-width characters;
- punctuation and spacing;
- model numbers, trademarks, and symbols.

Use the channel's current conventions when they differ from general locale conventions.

## Search-language research

Research query language from current data. Where supported, review:

- kanji, hiragana, katakana, and loanword variants;
- spacing and no-spacing variants;
- category and use-intent combinations;
- problem-led and feature-led phrasing;
- channel-specific query behavior.

Do not convert a translation glossary into a keyword strategy. Record evidence, date, intent, and allowed usage in the Market Evidence Registry.

## Native review

Before formal release, assign native review for:

- headline naturalness;
- ambiguity;
- tone;
- terminology;
- legal or claim wording;
- mobile comprehension;
- spoken-language elements when the copy will be read aloud.

Machine-generated or non-native copy remains `PROVISIONAL COPY` until this review is complete.

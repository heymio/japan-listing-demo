# Golden examples

These examples are category-neutral and teach creative principles rather than reusable final assets.

## Compact proof

**Weak:** a tiny product in a huge room plus a “compact” label. The room scale makes the product feel small but does not prove the shopper-relevant space relationship.

**Strong:** a controlled spatial relationship where furniture clearance, path width, or storage fit itself proves the compactness message.

## Mechanism explanation

**Weak:** abstract sci-fi effects around the product with no clear action/evidence chain.

**Strong:** one visible mechanism/action linked to one evidence object and one shopper benefit.

## One-image focus

**Weak:** three unrelated purchase reasons, multiple diagrams, and dense copy compete in one Gallery board.

**Strong:** one primary shopper decision, one dominant proof visual, and only supporting information needed to understand it.

## Product prominence

**Weak:** the product is secondary to decorative environment styling.

**Strong:** the product/evidence object has the visual weight appropriate to the slot, with realistic commercial lighting and low-interference context.

## Benchmark use

**Weak:** treat a previously good visual as a final reusable asset because the style matches.

**Strong:** learn from its hierarchy, lighting, realism, and composition while producing the new role-specific asset from the current Asset Packet.
